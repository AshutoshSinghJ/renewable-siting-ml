Can geography tell a solar farm from a wind farm?

A Random Forest trained on sunshine, wind, terrain and grid distance at 9,176 European renewable sites, then tested on Spain and Portugal, a region it never saw. It can also answer “I don't know”.

Problem statement

The question

Given a place where a renewable installation exists, can a model predict whether it is solar, wind or hybrid from geography alone, even in a region it has never seen, and say “I don't know” when it is unsure?

In scope
Onshore, utility-scale solar and wind farms. Testing on a whole held-out region.

Out of scope
Hydro, offshore wind, rooftop solar, economics, policy, and any claim that a place is “unsuitable”.

Solar

Only solar farms in the 5 km cell.

Wind

Only wind farms in the 5 km cell.

Hybrid

Both solar and wind farms in the same cell.

I don't know

Unsure, or the place is unlike anything seen in training.

The model answers “if something is built here, which type fits?”. It never says whether anything should be built, because it only ever learned from places where something was built.

No random splits

Neighbouring cells are near-copies, so a random split would inflate scores.

Tuning uses 5 folds made of whole 100 km blocks inside the training region.

All four models

Beyond Iberia: other regions and other continents

Each European region was held out in turn (trained on the other seven), and the Europe-trained model was applied unchanged to the USA, China and India. The model works where wind farms stand in much windier places than solar farms (southern Europe, the USA, largely China, where 81% of sites are wind farms) and fails where they do not (the British Isles, India). Across all eight European regions it is right of the time, so Iberia was an easier test than average. In India the model answers “I don't know” for 92% of cells.

Overfitting check: the Random Forest scores . A model that memorised its data would score near 100% on the data it has seen.

Would another model do better?

Spatial CV macro F1 on the training region only. Tree ensembles tie at the top; the limit is the data, not the algorithm.

What each feature group adds

Wind speed alone does most of the work. Sunshine and terrain add a little; grid distance adds almost nothing.

What drives the predictions (SHAP)

Reading the SHAP values

Solar: low wind speed is the strongest push towards Solar, and high sunshine adds to it.

Wind: the mirror image, driven mainly by high wind speed.

Hybrid: windy but not very sunny. That pattern comes from northern Germany, home to 213 of the 308 training Hybrids. Iberia is far sunnier (median GHI 4.6 against 3.1 kWh/m²/day), so the pattern does not carry over and Hybrid recall stays near zero.

Knowing when to abstain

Saying “I don't know”

Two rules. Rule A abstains when the top class probability is below 0.5. Rule B abstains when a cell's features are far from every training cell (the area-of-applicability idea of Meyer & Pebesma, 2021).

Verification

How we checked the results

Bad wind data caught and replaced

The first global wind raster did not match the official Global Wind Atlas (Gujarat coast 4.7 m/s against 6.6 m/s). It was replaced with official per-country files.

Island cells without grid data removed

Gridfinder has no lines on the Canary Islands and some small Mediterranean islands, so their grid distances (20 to 450 km) were wrong. Those 20 cells were dropped.

Fully reproducible

Running the whole pipeline again from scratch gave byte-identical outputs, with a fixed random seed of 42.

No leakage

No cell is in both regions, no land cover, roads or capacity feature is used, and the test region was scored exactly once.

Using the model on a new location

ASSUMPTION

If existing farms reflect well-informed siting decisions, the model recommends which type (Solar, Wind or Hybrid) fits a location if it is developed, and says “I don't know” when it is out of its depth.

Why it is reasonable

Developers study wind, sun, terrain and grid access before building, so real farms carry expert judgement. Sachit et al. (2022) uses the same idea.

Why it is only partly true

Land prices, subsidies and permits matter too: UK solar farms stand in windy places. Accuracy ranges from 35% (British Isles) to 92% (Italy).

How to use it

Read it as “which type fits if something is built”, never “build here”: the model never saw unsuitable land. Respect “I don't know”.

Limitations

  • Uneven coverage. OpenStreetMap is dominated by Europe, North America and East Asia.
  • 2020 snapshot. Capacity values are estimates and are used only as a filter.
  • Hybrid depends on the 5 km cell. It means co-location, not necessarily one joint project.
  • Few Hybrid examples. 308 for training and 25 for testing, mostly from Germany.
  • Climate shift. Iberia is sunnier and higher than the training region, which is the point of the test.
  • Type, not suitability. The model predicts the type given a farm exists, never whether one should be built.
